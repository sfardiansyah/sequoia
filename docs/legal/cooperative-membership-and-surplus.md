# Cooperative membership admission and surplus: what the statute says

Date: 2026-10-25. Terms: the statute says SHU (sisa hasil usaha); the plan
and code say **surplus**. The statute says jasa usaha and jasa modal; the plan and
code say **patronage** and **capital weight**. This note uses the statutory terms
because that is what counsel and the articles will use.

Prepared for counsel review. Not legal advice.

> **Update 2026-10-25.** The plan has moved from a 20/80 founder split and a 40%
> capital share to: (a) the same 70/30 `simpanan wajib`/`modal penyertaan` split
> for every member, with an optional additional founder `modal penyertaan`
> commitment; (b) `simpanan wajib` as `modal sendiri` equity, not a deposit;
> (c) a 20% endowment (`dana cadangan`) / 2.5% education / 52.5% patronage / 25%
> capital surplus split; (d) total equity (`simpanan wajib` + `modal penyertaan`)
> as the capital weight; and (e) an endowment limited to capital-loss absorption
> and approved development, with no exit settlement. The historical analysis
> below still explains the statutory constraints, but its 40% capital and 20/80
> founder questions are superseded. The live counsel questions are the 25%
> capital share, the 2.5% education allocation, the total-equity weight, and the
> founder’s extra `modal penyertaan` under Pasal 19(4) and Pasal 42. See ADR 0005
> and ADR 0006.

Two questions, both raised while designing the capital and distribution
contexts:

1. Does Indonesian koperasi law leave admission of members to the board, or to
   members?
2. Can the co-op distribute surplus in proportion to capital and participation,
   as the product plan assumed?

## 1. Admission

### Verdict

Admission of individual members is a **Pengurus (board) power**, exercised
within rules the **Anggaran Dasar (AD)** sets. Rapat Anggota (the members'
meeting) sets the rules, not the individual outcomes. A per-applicant member
vote is consistent with the statute only if the AD makes member approval a
*condition of membership*, rather than a discretionary veto over applicants who
already qualify.

### The provisions

Operative law is **UU 25/1992 tentang Perkoperasian**.

| Provision | Text | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pasal 5(1)(a) | "keanggotaan bersifat sukarela dan terbuka" | Membership is voluntary and **open** — a principle, not a procedure |
| Penjelasan Pasal 5(1)(a) | "sifat terbuka memiliki arti bahwa dalam keanggotaan tidak dilakukan pembatasan atau diskriminasi dalam bentuk apapun" | Open means **no restriction or discrimination of any kind** |
| Pasal 17(1) | "Anggota Koperasi adalah pemilik dan sekaligus pengguna jasa Koperasi" | A member is owner **and user** of the co-op's services |
| Pasal 18(1) | may be a member: any Indonesian citizen able to perform legal acts, or a Koperasi, "yang memenuhi persyaratan sebagaimana ditetapkan dalam Anggaran Dasar" | Eligibility criteria live in the **AD** |
| Pasal 18(2) | "Koperasi dapat memiliki anggota luar biasa yang persyaratan, hak, dan kewajiban keanggotaannya ditetapkan dalam Anggaran Dasar" | A second class: associate members (e.g. non-citizens, entities), terms in AD |
| Pasal 19(1) | membership "didasarkan pada kesamaan kepentingan ekonomi dalam lingkup usaha Koperasi" | A substantive eligibility test |
| Pasal 19(2) | membership "dapat diperoleh atau diakhiri setelah syarat sebagaimana diatur dalam Anggaran Dasar dipenuhi" | Acquisition is **conditional on AD requirements being met** |
| Pasal 19(3) | "Keanggotaan Koperasi tidak dapat dipindahtangankan" | Non-transferable |
| Pasal 22(1) | "Rapat Anggota merupakan pemegang kekuasaan tertinggi dalam Koperasi" | Members are the supreme authority |
| Pasal 23 | Rapat Anggota decides the AD, general policy, elections, work plan and budget, accounts, SHU, merger and dissolution | Admission is **absent** from the list |
| Pasal 24(3) | in a vote, "setiap anggota mempunyai hak satu suara" | One member, one vote |
| Pasal 30(2)(b) | Pengurus "memutuskan penerimaan dan penolakan anggota baru serta pemberhentian anggota sesuai dengan ketentuan dalam Anggaran Dasar" | **Admission, refusal, and termination are board powers** |

### The three layers

1. **The AD sets the requirements.** Citizenship and legal capacity (Pasal 18),
   common economic interest (Pasal 19(1)), and whatever else the AD specifies.
   The AD is adopted and amended by Rapat Anggota (Pasal 12(1), Pasal 23(a)).
2. **Pengurus applies them case by case** (Pasal 30(2)(b)). The statute gives the
   board the grant, the refusal, and the termination.
3. **Rapat Anggota oversees** — it elects and can remove Pengurus (Pasal 23(c)),
   demands accountability (Pasal 25), and amends the AD so the criteria change.

Rapat Anggota is the supreme authority (Pasal 22(1)), but that authority is
exercised by *setting the rules*, not by adjudicating individual applicants.

### The constraint on refusing an applicant

Pasal 5(1)(a) and its official explanation prohibit "pembatasan atau
diskriminasi dalam bentuk apapun". A refusal of someone who meets the AD
requirements therefore needs a lawful basis — KYC/AML failure, sanctions, an AD
criterion, or a condition the applicant has not satisfied. It cannot be taste.

Historical signal: the since-annulled UU 17/2012 made exactly this an
enforcement trigger. Its Pasal 98(1)(a) allowed the Minister to inspect a
Koperasi that "membatasi keanggotaan atau menolak permohonan untuk menjadi
Anggota atas orang perseorangan yang telah memenuhi persyaratan keanggotaan
sebagaimana ditetapkan dalam Anggaran Dasar". That law is dead, but it shows the
regulator's instinct.

### If members should vote on admissions

**Statutorily clean route:** put it in the AD. If the AD states that admission
requires member approval, or admission at a Rapat Anggota, or ratification of
the year's admissions, then approval is a condition of membership under
Pasal 18(1) and 19(2) — the applicant does not yet meet every requirement, so
nobody is refusing a qualified applicant. Apply it uniformly and record the vote
in the admission record.

**Unclean route:** leave the AD silent and let members veto board-approved
applicants. That is Pengurus delegating its own statutory power without an AD
basis, and it collides with the open-membership principle.

**Middle route** that keeps the statutory allocation intact: Pengurus admits, and
Rapat Anggota ratifies the year's admissions en bloc or hears appeals.

## 2. SHU and the capital key

### Verdict

Sharing the entire surplus pro-rata by capital is **not** the koperasi model.
The primary key must be participation ("jasa usaha"); a return on capital is
permitted but limited. The compliant shape is a dual key with AD-fixed
proportions — which is what ADR 0005 now specifies.

### The provisions

| Provision | Text | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pasal 5(1)(c) | "pembagian sisa hasil usaha dilakukan secara adil sebanding dengan besarnya jasa usaha masing-masing anggota" | SHU is shared by **service**, not capital |
| Pasal 5(1)(d) | "pemberian balas jasa yang terbatas terhadap modal" | Return on capital is **limited**, i.e. secondary and capped |
| Pasal 23(f) | Rapat Anggota sets "pembagian sisa hasil usaha" | The proportions are a member decision |
| Pasal 17(1) | member is owner **and user** | A co-op of passive investors sits badly with this |

The doctrine matters here: when the Constitutional Court annulled UU 17/2012
(Putusan 28/PUU-XI/2013), the public argument was that the law had given the
co-op a corporate spirit and lost the family principle. A capital-weighted
distribution is the same critique in miniature.

### What this means for the model

The plan's project-investment return is not obviously "sisa hasil usaha" at all
— SHU is the surplus of the co-op's business with its members. Two readings:

- **Reading A (the one adopted):** the co-op's business is pooling member capital
  to fund projects; placing capital at risk is the member's use of the co-op, so
  capital is a legitimate jasa usaha measure *and* a limited jasa modal measure.
  Defensible if the AD says so and it applies uniformly.
- **Reading B:** members are investors, not patrons; the return is a return on
  capital and the vehicle should be a PT holding the pool, with the koperasi as
  the membership and governance layer.

Reading A is cheaper by far; Reading B is cleaner economically and roughly
doubles the system. This is the single most important question for counsel.

### Open points for counsel

1. Does an AD defining jasa usaha as the member's transaction value with the
   co-op satisfy Pasal 5(1)(c)? What if there are no transactions in a year?
2. Is a 25% capital share (32.3% of member distributions) “terbatas” within
   Pasal 5(1)(d)? What ceiling has been accepted in practice or by the ministry?
3. Must the AD allocate to `dana cadangan` and `dana pendidikan`; does the 20%
   endowment as `dana cadangan` plus the 2.5% retained education fund satisfy
   those obligations?
4. A `dana cadangan` that absorbs members' capital losses before their own
   capital and funds approved development — is it permissible, and how is it
   presented in the AD and the financial statements?
5. Does `simpanan wajib`, held as `modal sendiri` with a 100% redemption reserve
   and no member lending, trigger Koperasi Simpan Pinjam classification (see
   Permenkop 8/2023 on KSP/KSPPS)? The risk is lower than a withdrawable savings
   product but depends on the actual operations.
6. Does the same 70/30 split for every member, plus an additional founder `modal
   penyertaan` commitment, satisfy Pasal 19(4) and the open-membership principle?
7. Admission by member vote, if wanted: does it need to be in the AD, and does
   Rapat Anggota ratification satisfy the open-membership principle more safely?

## Status of the law (re-check before mainnet)

- **UU 25/1992 is the operative statute.** UU 17/2012 replaced it, then
  **Putusan MK 28/PUU-XI/2013 annulled UU 17/2012 in its entirety** and revived
  UU 25/1992 "untuk sementara waktu" pending a replacement.
- Amended by UU 11/2020 (Cipta Kerja) and UU 6/2023. The amendments touched a
  handful of articles, not the membership core. Verify against a consolidated
  text: the amendment list here comes from a DPR legislative discussion, not
  from the statute itself.
- A **fourth amendment (RUU Perubahan Keempat atas UU 25/1992)** is in process:
  RDPU from February 2025, Tingkat I work meeting on 17 June 2026, still a draft
  in the latest reporting available (August 2026). It covers membership,
  institutional form, capital, and supervision. Re-check before mainnet.
